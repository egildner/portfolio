Building an AI System to Run
L&D Operations Without Manual Overhead

How I designed and deployed an AI-powered triage and coordination system for L&D product development, one that manages a full workflow end-to-end without human intervention.

System L&D Product Development System
Domain L&D Operations
Tool AI (Claude Project)
Outcome Zero-intervention triage

01 · Situation

L&D product development was growing faster than manual coordination could keep up.

As the education and training portfolio expanded, the process of triaging, routing, and coordinating L&D product development requests became a consistent friction point. Projects came in at different stages, with different stakeholders, different scopes, and no standardized intake pathway. Coordination happened in emails, comments, and memory. Nothing about that scales.

I saw this as an AI design problem, not just a process problem. The question was: could a well-scoped AI system replace the manual coordination layer entirely. Not by cutting corners, but by executing the workflow with more consistency and speed than a human could while managing everything else?

The goal wasn't automation for automation's sake. It was removing low-value coordination work so the professional responsible for it could spend time on design, strategy, and stakeholder relationships instead.

02 · How the System Works

Intake. Triage. Population. Handoff, with no manual steps in between.

The system is a scoped AI assistant configured with custom instructions, workflow logic, and intake sequencing specific to L&D product development. It functions as a triage and coordination assistant for the Education and Training Program Manager role.

Intake
Structured intake questioning

The system asks the right questions in the right order, surfacing what's needed to route a project correctly without back-and-forth or missing fields.

Triage
Workflow routing and prioritization

Based on intake responses, the system routes the project through the appropriate pathway, applying coordination logic that would otherwise live in a person's head.

Population
Field completion and documentation

The system fills in the required fields, populates structured templates, and adds comments, producing a complete, actionable record without manual entry.

Handoff
Program manager-ready output

The output is ready for ETPM review and action, not a rough draft that needs cleanup. The system handles the coordination; the human handles the judgment.

03 · Design Decisions

The thinking behind a system that actually works in production.

Tight scope produces reliable output. The system does one thing: L&D product development triage and coordination. That constraint is what makes it reliable. A broadly scoped AI assistant produces broadly unreliable output. Scoping precisely is a design skill, not a limitation.

Sequencing questions intentionally. The intake logic is not a form. It's a conversation architecture, designed so each answer informs what gets asked next, the way an experienced coordinator would move through a project, not in a fixed linear order.

Building in evaluative discipline. After live testing, an open design question remained: is the comment-based input mechanism the most effective approach, or is there a more efficient structure? Holding that question rather than over-optimizing is intentional. A system that works should be evaluated before it is changed.

04 · Before & After

What changed when the system existed.

Before: Manual Coordination

  • Intake through ad hoc emails and comment threads
  • Routing logic lived in the coordinator's memory
  • Fields missed, steps skipped, follow-up required
  • Coordination overhead competed with design time
  • No standard output. Every record looked different

After: AI-Powered System

  • Intake is structured and sequenced automatically
  • Routing follows embedded workflow logic every time
  • All fields populated, comments added, record complete
  • Coordinator time freed for design, strategy, and relationships
  • Consistent, reusable output across every project
