01 · Situation

The organization needed to move from AI curiosity to AI fluency.

Studio 22, an internal AI innovation group spearheaded by the Chief Digital Officer, was charged with more than building tools internally. It needed to bring the broader organization along. That meant designing a learning experience that wasn't a lecture or a vendor demo. Something staff could actually do, in real time, with real stakes.

As a Studio 22 member and instructional designer, I took ownership of Room A for the all-staff session. My job was to design an activity that would give participants hands-on AI experience, require genuine collaboration, and produce something they could reflect on and pitch. Not a simulation. A real build, on the clock.

The goal was simple: leave the session with a memory of building something with AI, not just watching someone else do it.

02 · Challenges

High variability. High stakes. One shot to get it right.

Facilitating AI learning for a mixed-skill audience at an all-staff level introduced several design and delivery challenges simultaneously.

01 Wildly different starting points

Participants ranged from AI skeptics to daily users. The activity had to work for both without alienating either.

02 Compressed time window

The full cycle, brainstorm, build, stress-test, and pitch, had to happen in one session. There was no room for confusion or slow starts.

03 Last-minute scope change

The format shifted from an in-person all-staff event to a Zoom capstone session. The activity design had to flex without losing its energy or intent.

04 Making AI feel real, not staged

Participants needed to experience AI as a genuine thinking partner. The facilitation approach had to create that authenticity from the start.

03 · Approach

Structure that created momentum. Constraints that sparked creativity.

I designed and facilitated the Five Person Challenge, a structured rapid-build activity purpose-built for this session. The format gave teams enough constraint to move fast and enough freedom to be genuinely creative.

1
Define the challenge

Teams identified a real healthcare learning need, something specific enough to build toward, broad enough to explore with AI tools.

2
Build with Perplexity

Using Perplexity, teams rapidly built out an AI-powered learning concept. The constraint: use the tool, don't just describe what you would build.

3
Stress-test the concept

Teams pressure-tested their build against real questions: Who is this for? What does it actually teach? Where would it break? This modeled evaluative thinking as a core AI skill.

4
60-second pitch

Each team presented their concept in 60 seconds, short enough to force clarity, public enough to create real investment in the outcome.

5
Virtual pivot: same structure, adapted delivery

When the format shifted from in-person to a Zoom capstone, I adapted the facilitation approach without rebuilding the activity design. The core sequence held. The energy translated.

I also drafted the program model for the Six Trends Education Series, a companion initiative contextualizing the session within broader organizational direction, a model that required its own pivot when scope shifted mid-development.

04 · Outcomes

A session that worked. And a strategic signal that came out of it.

Participants left having done something with AI, not just heard about it. The activity generated genuine engagement across skill levels, and the 60-second pitch format created energy that's hard to manufacture in a corporate learning context.

Beyond the session itself, the concepts that emerged from the Five Person Challenge became input for a larger strategic conversation at the organization, about where its product and platform strategy could go next. That's a rare outcome for a single learning session.
